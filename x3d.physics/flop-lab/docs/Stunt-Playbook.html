01 / A good first five minutes

Learn one thing.
Then make a splendid mess.

Showtime adds a playful score layer to the existing physics playground. The controlled hero runs and jumps; F hands control to an eleven-body ragdoll. Directional puffs let you improvise during the flop. The show recognizes a mixture of actual motion and actual course interactions, then banks a chain when you recover.

  1. Try an ordinary jump and flop.Jump with Space. Press F soon after takeoff while rising. Watch the contrast between an upright hero and a limp body. The first success is understanding that transition, not achieving an enormous number.
  2. Give the noodle a nudge.While limp, use Q for a directional puff. Hold a movement direction to choose its direction; with no direction held, the default helps you rise. A new flop has a small supply. Spend one puff, observe the result, then try another at a different moment.
  3. Finish the sentence.Recover with F after the brief recovery delay, or let the ragdoll settle. The show banks on recovery, including F in midair; it does not require a grounded landing. Respawning discards its unbanked chain. This makes the recovery part of the trick rather than merely the button before the next trick.
  4. Add one different ingredient.Try a bounce pad, a grapple release, a fan, water or a cannon. Variety gives the show something new to recognize. Repeatable collisions with the same floor are useful physics observations, but they should not become an unlimited score printer.
  5. Read one prompt; keep one experiment.The show prompts are open invitations. Choose a small objective, try it, and examine its progress. Save the X3D level when you alter the course. A funny result becomes a useful experiment when you can identify what made it different.
Two games, two meanings. Showtime points are a local play toy. Expedition medals compare the original challenge rules within their declared course session. Using a puff marks an Expedition attempt as practice; it does not quietly turn an assisted flight into an equivalent stock medal. You can enjoy the show and investigate the museum at the same time.

The current keyboard controls are Space to jump, F to flop or recover, Q to puff, and E/C for an upright air dash. Touch includes a puff button; optional gamepad input maps Y to puff. Use the on-screen hints for the complete mapping. Clicking the course gives gameplay keys focus; typing into an editor should remain typing. Sound is a separate opt-in. Quiet play should retain the information needed to understand a stunt.

Take the Giggleworks tour.

The new Giggleworks Carnival is a seeded course built from existing source prefabs. Its broad runways put familiar mechanisms into a playful sequence. The six checkpoints are invitations to explore rather than a claim that one human playtest has completed every route.

02 / How a physics game earns a laugh

Give the player a plan.
Let the world answer.

A useful slapstick loop has anticipation, a readable reversal, and recovery. A player sees a ring and plans a graceful swing. The hook catches, the release is slightly early, a knee finds a plank, and the eventual landing has a suspicious amount of confidence. The design aims to make the intended action recognizable inside the surprising result.

Unstructured randomness can weaken that relationship. If the game chooses an unrelated outcome, the player cannot learn a new route or claim authorship of the disaster. Here, the physics and authored controls create the motion; the comic captions respond to the event. A caption should supply punctuation, not substitute for a cause.

Heuristic / Preserve intention

Forgive the entrance.

Jump buffering, coyote time and readable movement help the player reach the interesting decision. An input missed by a few display frames is rarely the most entertaining part of a stunt. Keep the hero responsive enough that relinquishing control feels like a choice.

Heuristic / Exaggerate the consequence

Let bodies tell the joke.

A jointed ragdoll supplies follow-through: a chest rotates, a leg arrives late, and a landing becomes a small sequence. Clear silhouettes and an unobstructed camera matter more than another layer of particles over the decisive collision.

Heuristic / Keep the next attempt close

Recovery is generosity.

Quick retry and local exploration reduce the cost of curiosity. A failed route can still teach a swing timing, earn a familiar caption, or reveal a mechanism. The player should not need to wait through a lecture before trying the funny idea again.

Heuristic / Treat surprise as evidence

Name the useful accident.

A bonk, a flip or a puff should correspond to an observed game event. A score that rewards button presses without the intended result teaches a different game. Good feedback makes the surprising event legible enough to repeat deliberately.

A satisfying failure leaves the player thinking, “I know what to try,” even while the dummy is still folding.

Readable failure also needs room. Broad landing areas invite improvisation; tight precision sections create focus. Alternate them. Put a low-risk demonstration before a combination, let the camera reveal a tempting destination, and offer a safe return route. A carnival becomes richer through relationships among familiar mechanisms, not simply through more simultaneous objects.

03 / A grammar for ridiculous motion

Link causes.
Reward a complete performance.

The show observes the live fixed-step simulation. It can recognize motion milestones and interactions, assemble them into a chain, and attach an authored multiplier. The categories are game design. A high score is not a calibrated assessment of athletic skill, a physical truth, or a verified leaderboard result.

IngredientUseful causal questionWhat to avoid rewarding
Air and rotationDid this active ragdoll actually remain airborne or make a meaningful turn?Upright waiting, an angle copied from an earlier stunt, or small back-and-forth noise counted as full flips.
ImpactWas there a sufficiently distinct collision in a new motion context?A resting contact producing points every tick, or duplicated events replayed as new impacts.
SetupDid a legal dash, hook or pad launch actually contribute to this run?Pressing a refused ability key or retaining setup credit forever after unrelated travel.
EnvironmentDid the active stunt interact with the fan, water or target?Counting a region that exists in the file but was never entered, or borrowing an earlier stunt's result.
RecoveryDid this stunt produce a new completed record?Banking the same completion object twice, or banking a chain after death or source replacement.

The authored arithmetic.

Awarded base points accumulate in the chain. The current multiplier is min(4, 1 + floor(distinct families / 2)); the current chain score is the rounded base total times that multiplier, capped at 99,999. The multiplier applies to the whole pending chain, so adding a new family can increase the value of earlier ingredients. There are at most 24 credited trick awards per chain; specific motion and interaction categories have tighter bounds.

Air awards occur at 0.35, 0.8, 1.5 and 2.5 seconds of accumulated airborne time within one flop. Rotation awards use up to three net full chest turns, rather than accumulating every small reversal. Travelling bonks require impact speed above 5 m/s, prior airborne time, a different contacted body, separation in time and at least 1.2 m of chest travel from the previous credited position. These are game-design gates, not biomechanical or damage thresholds.

Why variety and limits belong together.

A multiplier can make a varied route feel like a composition: approach, release, turn, correction, landing. If every repeated event has the same unlimited value, the optimal route often becomes a tedious exploit. Diminishing returns, category limits and explicit reset rules keep the more interesting behavior valuable. They also make the score easier to inspect: the player can see which ingredient changed the result.

There are several score domains in the lab. The original stunt card summarizes distance, airtime, spins, impacts and speed. Showtime adds its own playful chain and session progression. Expedition tracks its own challenge objective and medal eligibility. These systems can coexist because the interface names them. Do not compare numbers from different domains as if they measured one quantity.

Use a prompt as a lens, not a lock.

An open prompt focuses attention on one relationship without closing the rest of the course. A player can investigate an airborne correction while another prefers a graceful landing or a conspicuous collision. A prompt communicates progress during the current chain and completes only after a qualifying bank. The seven prompts range from The Wobbly Debut (0.35 seconds of accumulated limp airtime) to Express Biscuit Delivery (six metres), Pocket Weather System (one puff), The Spin Cycle (one net full turn), Three-Course Tumble (three families), Bonk Appétit (two distinct moving bonks) and The Grand Wobble (1,000 style points).

Nine stickers form a compact memory of playful accomplishments: Certified Wobbler, Chairperson of the Air, Laundry Astronaut, Professional Floor Consultant, Distinguished Wet Biscuit, Department of Everything, Puffin in Disguise, Extremely Qualified Noodle and Please Remain Unseated. Its role is to celebrate a different kind of event, not demand an endless series of nearly identical grinds. Session totals, best chain, earned stickers and completed prompts remain across world or mission rebuilds within the current page session. Pending chains reset. These totals deliberately mix playful exploration across courses; they are not course-comparable records. Persistent or shared records would need a separate versioned identity and storage contract.

Keep the accounting inspectable. The source owner is src/showtime.js. Read its constants and event conditions when tuning the show. The display is a consumer of those results; it should not mint score because a particle rendered, a sound finished, or a pose was reviewed.

04 / An authored impulse, not a hidden miracle

Three small chances
to negotiate with gravity.

The ragdoll puff is a deliberately arcade-like ability. A new flop provides three charges, a 0.35-second cooldown and a 6 m/s velocity-change magnitude per legal puff. A legal puff gives the active ragdoll bodies the same change in linear velocity. It does not rewrite the X3D starting layout or pretend to be an actuator derived from the dummy's anatomy.

That equal change has a useful interpretation. For active bodies with total mass M and centre-of-mass velocity V, a common velocity increment u adds total linear momentum M·u. It preserves each pair's relative linear velocity at the instant it is applied. Contacts and joint corrections in later substeps can then turn the changed approach into a very different landing.

v′i = vi + u
Δp = Σ miu = M u
ΔK = M V · u + ½ M |u|²

The eleven declared ragdoll masses sum to 10.4 kg in the current game model. A 6 m/s common boost therefore adds 62.4 kg·m/s of momentum in its chosen direction; from zero centre-of-mass velocity it adds 187.2 J of translational kinetic energy. These are arithmetic consequences of the authored model, not measurements of a human or a tiny rocket. The energy expression follows by expanding the squared velocities before and after a common boost. Its sign depends on direction relative to the current motion. An opposing puff can remove translational kinetic energy; an aligned puff can add it. Either way, it is an external authored control action. Do not use a conservation-of-energy claim that omits the puff's work.

Normalize direction before choosing strength.

If diagonal input simply added the horizontal and vertical full-strength values, it would produce a larger impulse than a single-axis input. Normalizing a nonzero direction makes the chosen strength independent of that geometry. The zero-input case needs an explicit helpful default rather than division by zero. Refused inputs must consume no charge and create no credited event.

Timing changes the contact story.

An early upward puff can buy time for rotation. A lateral puff near a ledge can change which body meets it first. A late opposing puff can reduce travel but improve the recovery. The ability therefore supports several strategies without needing many buttons. Its limited supply makes the decision visible within one flop; the cooldown prevents immediate repeated applications. Recovering and beginning a new flop reloads all three puffs. Repeated recovery/flop cycles can therefore extend flight by design. This is an open arcade toy, not an anti-cheat or globally fuel-limited flight system.

A puff does not directly make every joint stiffer or reset the body's angular history. If the final rotation changes, inspect the later contacts, changed flight duration and player wiggle before attributing it to a hidden torque. The engine still uses its existing compliant position corrections and pill approximations. This extension does not convert it into a full accumulated-multiplier XPBD implementation.

05 / Tinkering with a smile

Keep the joke.
Change one explanation.

Experiment A / Correction timing

Early puff, late puff.

Start the same course with the same seed. Use the same jump and flop, then apply one upward puff at two different fixed ticks. Compare chest height, horizontal travel and recovery time. A hand-timed browser attempt is a useful feel test; a named input trace is a stronger numerical comparison.

Keep: source, seed, fixed ticks, input direction and the moment of puff application. Compare motion separately from score.

Experiment B / Relative motion

Watch the joints after the boost.

Pause before a puff, single-step through it, and inspect linear velocities and contact overlays. Equal velocity increments do not imply equal final trajectories after contact. Compare a clear airborne boost with a boost beside a wall to isolate the role of the environment.

Keep: active-body set, pre/post velocities and subsequent contact state. Avoid inferring torque from a later picture alone.

Experiment C / Variety

One trick repeated; three linked.

Try a deliberately repetitive route, then a route that mixes setup, motion and recovery. Inspect category recognition and the multiplier. If an immobile floor interaction beats a composed route indefinitely, preserve that reproducer as a score-system defect.

Keep: awarded event types, event identities, pending points, bank amount and any repetition limits reached.

Experiment D / Course authorship

Move the invitation.

Export the course, then move one ring, pad or landing platform through the source tools. Does the intended route become more readable, or merely more difficult? Compare the visible invitation, the reachable landing area and the cost of recovery. An obstacle is also a teaching cue.

Keep: before/after X3D files, changed part identity, route hypothesis and observations. Edited attempts are laboratory practice.

Measure fun with people as well as instruments.

A test can establish that a refused puff consumes no charge. It cannot establish that a new player understands the button label. Watch where people hesitate, whether they can explain a failed attempt, how often they retry voluntarily, and whether they find a new route without being told. Keep those observations separate from CPU timings and deterministic input checks.

Useful playtest questions are concrete: “What did you expect that ring to do?” “What would you change on your next attempt?” “Which number helped you?” A person smiling at the first ragdoll fall and a person studying a stable constraint are both valid audiences. Let the interface offer a quick playful loop and a deeper explanation without forcing one rhythm on everyone.

06 / Punctuation with an off switch

Celebrate the event.
Leave the cause visible.

Comic callouts, confetti and synthesized sounds form a presentation layer. Their job is to make a recognized event easier to feel and remember. They do not change the simulation, generate score or replace the plain-language state in the interface. The same stunt should remain understandable with sound disabled and motion effects reduced.

Different events need different emphasis. A small correction deserves a small acknowledgement. A banked chain deserves a clear moment of closure. A rare accomplishment can be louder in visual hierarchy without requiring a louder audio signal. If every collision triggers a finale, the player loses the distinction between setup, consequence and success.

Bound the celebration.

Transient effects need a lifetime, a count budget and an identity. Duplicated event delivery should not double the celebration. Pausing, reviewing an old pose or returning from a background tab should not unleash a backlog of obsolete bursts. The celebration layer accepts at most 16 events per call and retains at most 200 particles, two cards, 512 deduplication identities and 12 audio voices. Display-step advancement is capped at 0.05 seconds and rendering pixel ratio at 1.5. These are work budgets, not measured frame-rate promises. Hidden or paused delivery clears stale effects.

Reduced-motion behavior should remove unnecessary movement rather than merely shorten it. Camera shake, fast bursts and animated emphasis are separate choices. The operating-system reduced-motion preference is always respected; the visible control can request additional reduction. It cannot override a system request for reduction. The current reduced-motion path retains text cards while suppressing animated particles and world bursts/shake. Cosmetic party hat, pilot goggles and noodle antenna choices change presentation, not collision geometry.

Make sound an invitation.

The optional sound layer uses local synthesis rather than downloaded clips or a model call. Its enable action belongs to an explicit user gesture. Browser audio can still be unavailable or suspended, and the interface should describe that state. The inherited automatic beep path has been removed. The sound control or M explicitly enables the new synth, and its status reports the actual audio state. The sounds are chirps, boops and whistles, not recorded laughter or generated speech.

Humour should be on the player's side. Tease the improbable landing, not the person's competence. “The floor has accepted your proposal” gives the mishap a friendly interpretation. A failure message that shames the player discourages the experiment the game wants them to try.

07 / A stunt is also a model you can inspect

X3D lets the playground
outlive one renderer.

The immediate advantage of the X3D boundary is concrete: a course has named bodies, dimensions, joint references and regions that can be exported, inspected, changed and independently checked. The gameplay engine and the 2.5D renderer consume a common source model instead of treating renderer objects as the only surviving description of the world.

ArtifactUseful identityBoundary to preserve
X3D courseExact source bytes, node/part IDs, units and bridge version.Starting level specification; not every current body pose or controller timer.
Show rulesEngine source, authored ability values, scoring and prompt policy.Application behavior; not automatically normative X3D semantics.
Input traceOrdered inputs, fixed tick, starting state and seed.A reproducibility candidate that still needs a declared host comparison.
Ghost or pose reviewRecorded presentation samples and their scope.Visual comparison; not a complete state checkpoint.
FeedbackEvent sequence and presentation preferences.Observer output; it should not alter physics or scoring.

Custom Flop metadata retains application-specific meaning such as prefabs, compliant joints and game-region interpretation. A normal X3D viewer can inspect the starting arrangement without becoming the Flop Lab engine. Schema validity helps establish structural correctness; it does not establish native physics parity, identical collisions or the same medal rules.

Keep generator ownership visible.

A procedural recipe and its expanded bodies are related artifacts. If an editor changes a body directly but later regenerates the recipe, that direct change may disappear. Preserve a clear rule for generated content and explicit overrides. Stable part identities make source-to-runtime selection, diagnostics and future route annotations far more useful.

This is also the route to better authoring. A future challenge card could refer to named source regions and a versioned show policy, rather than hard-code screen coordinates. Another renderer could display the same starting course and indicate the features it supports. Each mapping should retain units, orientation, reference identity and declared losses. Those are future adapter goals, not completed native-engine equivalence.

08 / What the green checks can and cannot say

Keep delight subjective.
Keep mechanics testable.

The automated controls for this extension live beside its source. They can test charge limits, refused actions, reset boundaries, score causality, duplicate handling and whether neutral observation leaves an input trace's physical poses unchanged. The independent audit records eight actual-Sim integration controls. One ordinary stock-spawn trace settles 40 ticks, jumps on tick 41, flops on tick 46, requests upward puffs on ticks 48, 72 and 96, then recovers on tick 116. It banks 510 toy points with 1.167 seconds of observed limp airtime and the three-puff sticker. That establishes one attainable loop on the named local engine, not a human success rate. The final validation record scopes the delivered version and any further checks.

Those checks are different from direct browser observations. An actual browser can reveal clipped controls, an unreadable callout, unavailable audio or a fallback renderer. A hardware-GPU benchmark needs its own hardware and work definition. A successful gamepad mapping requires actual input testing, not merely a button table in source. Read the delivery's validation record for the measured scope.

The engine advances physical state on a fixed simulation tick; the renderer advances presentation on display time. Slow motion changes the relationship between those clocks. A count of ticks, CPU milliseconds per tick, display cadence and audio latency are different quantities. A celebration should not become part of the physical input merely because it uses a wall clock.

Historical cross-host receipts remain historical. A pose fingerprint omits velocities and hidden game state; an attractive old frame is not sufficient to resume a deterministic continuation. The current review path returns to retained live state. No shared verified leaderboard, complete-state checkpoint, general native X3D physics parity or formal Loom profile acceptance is established by this expansion.

  • src/celebration.js: bounded visual effects and optional synthesized sound.
  • src/app.js: live-step integration, input focus, source lifecycle and presentation controls.
  • test/showtime.test.mjs and test/showtime-integration.test.mjs: controller and actual-Sim controls.
  • docs/SHOWTIME-AUDIT.md, docs/STUNT-FOLDBACK.md and the final validation record: findings, repaired cases and evidence boundaries.

A long-lived Flop Lab can be both a joyful game and a serious development instrument. A player follows a spectacular idea; an author turns that idea into a named mechanism; a reviewer can inspect what the mechanism actually does. The next useful feature is the one that creates a new choice and makes its consequences easier to understand.
