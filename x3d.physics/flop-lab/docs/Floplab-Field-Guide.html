01 / A good first ten minutes

Play first.
Then choose one question.

Flop Lab is a 2.5D side-scrolling physics playground. Its solver moves bodies in the XY plane; a three.js bridge turns that same model into an extruded neon world. You can run and wall-jump as a controlled hero, become an eleven-body ragdoll, hook rings, ride fields and fire a dummy cannon.

When WebGL is unavailable, the interface can use a clearly labeled Canvas2D view. That fallback preserves a useful control and inspection surface, but it does not demonstrate the three-dimensional renderer or GPU performance.

The Expedition edition adds authored objectives and a clearer path from a playful result to an inspectable experiment. Its mission bars, medal times, dash impulse and visual style are design choices. Their existence is not a measurement of real human motion or a calibrated material model. That distinction is useful: game feel can be tuned confidently when it does not masquerade as a physical fact.

  1. Open the local build.Use the supplied launcher or serve the project through loopback HTTP. The upgraded distribution bundles its renderer; a model API and external asset service are not required for local play.
  2. Start with Feel or First Flight.Jump, then press F promptly while rising. The bounce pad offers an optional bigger launch. Recover with F after the brief limp interval. Leave the solver at its stock settings until the controls feel familiar.
  3. Choose one observable question.For example: does more spring compliance produce more extension at the same load? Does changing friction alter the sliding threshold? Predict a result before touching a slider.
  4. Keep the model and compare one change.Save the X3D level, note the tuning, and repeat the same action. Record the relevant outcome with its units and conditions. An amusing accident becomes a reusable lesson when you can explain what changed.
Local curator: built-in commands and experiment tools remain useful without a model connection. A local explanation or authored command is not a live response from the historical model named by the original checkpoint. Read the current connection label instead of inferring service availability from a tab title.

02 / An open routebook

Objectives give the playground
a reason to surprise you.

Freeplay remains available. The authored missions focus attention on distinct interactions rather than locking the laboratory behind a linear progression. A mission is available when its needed course features exist. Editing, tuning or teleporting during a run marks it as practice, so a laboratory intervention does not quietly become a comparable medal result.

Bronze, silver and gold use authored time bars. Compare like with like: the same course, stock tuning, starting condition and control policy. A practice run can be more educational than a fast eligible run because it lets you isolate a variable. The interface keeps those roles separate.

The air dash is a game mechanic.

The dash offers one airborne charge, a short cooldown and ground recharge. Its directional impulse uses your movement direction, or facing when no direction is held. It is unavailable while hooked, limp or inside the cannon. Use E or C on a keyboard; read the touch and controller hints shown by the current interface. The impulse is an authored control action, not a force inferred from a physically modeled actuator.

The best eligible completed trace can be drawn as a ghost. It is a bounded set of visual pose samples, not a second active body and not proof of deterministic input replay. The current collection samples at 15 Hz and retains at most 2,400 samples. A ghost is useful for route timing and comparison while the underlying mission record names how the run was qualified.

Seven questions for the solver.

Use the hall buttons to explore quickly. A teleport is a navigation convenience for learning, and a practice intervention for a timed challenge. The two uses can coexist when the interface names them.

Explore the world forge.

The Seven Halls preserves the original museum. Grapple Skyline emphasizes vertical travel, lifts and rings; Cascade Foundry stages dominoes, a seesaw, pegboard and a target tower; Tidal Conservatory combines wind, rafts and compliant structures. The three alternate recipes use an authored integer seed to choose declared part parameters. The recipe and seed are useful inputs, while an exported X3D file retains the exact generated layout. Generated worlds start as laboratory practice rather than quietly joining the stock medal table.

Use Follow for ordinary travel, Overview to read a layout, and Free to inspect a mechanism. Alt-drag or middle-drag pans the camera. Low, Balanced and High quality settings, shake and overlays are presentation controls; changing them should not change the solver model. If you use a controller, enable it explicitly and focus the canvas: the displayed mapping is left stick for movement, A for jump, B for flop, X for dash and RB for hook.

Click a body to identify its source part. The selected-part panel can frame it, show its recipe parameters, apply a validated edit, delete it or undo the last source edit. Those changes rebuild an authored specification and mark the run as practice. Keep a source export before an experiment when you want a durable comparison, rather than relying on one undo slot as an archive.

04 / Experiments that teach something specific

Change one input.
Keep the counterexample.

Experiment A / Gravity and feel

Follow an ordinary body.

Drop a ball from the same position at two world-gravity settings. Measure its vertical position after the same number of fixed ticks. Then repeat with the hero. The hero controller compensates world gravity using its own feel target, falling multiplier and jump-cut behavior. The slider therefore has different meanings for the two subjects.

Keep: body identity, starting state, gravity, fixed ticks and measured position. Describe the hero's difference as authored control.

Experiment B / Softness

Inspect displacement, not wobble alone.

Keep the lattice, load and clock policy fixed. Increase spring compliance, observe extension and settling, then change substeps separately. Wobble depends on inertia, damping, coupling and contact as well as compliance. A visually larger oscillation does not isolate elastic stiffness.

Keep: compliance, substeps, iterations, displacement at a named time and peak excursion.

Experiment C / Contact ladder

Start with rest.

Inspect one crate, then a short stack, then the larger pile. Separate sideways creep, tilt and residual speed. A contact change that improves one impact can destabilize a resting face. Repeat with a deliberately altered solver preset so the observation has a meaningful failing twin.

Keep: fixture, duration, maximum tilt, drift and speed; judge the mutant by the same stated limit.

Experiment D / Cannon sweep

Preserve the whole specimen.

Run an angle or power sweep through the local experiment tool. Each row starts a fresh simulation. The spinner phase, moving obstacles, launch configuration and collision path matter. The textbook 45-degree maximum-range result belongs to a simpler vacuum trajectory and is not a universal answer for this course.

Keep: exact level, engine, parameter values, fixed-step horizon, output units and one caveat.

A good comparison predicts what should remain unchanged. When studying friction, do not also rebuild the geometry. When studying a camera, do not replace the solver. When studying an importer, compare the parsed model before the resulting pose fingerprints. This makes the first mismatch a diagnostic rather than another mystery.

05 / The engine you can read

A small solver is valuable
because its assumptions are visible.

The application advances at a fixed outer tick of dt = 1/60 seconds. Each physical tick uses n substeps, so the local step is h = dt/n. The engine predicts motion, detects contacts, solves joints and penetration, applies friction, reconstructs velocity, adds restitution and damps selected joint motion. It also updates the simple water surface.

Understand the compliance denominator.

For a scalar distance constraint, this code computes a correction multiplier of the form lambda = C / (w + compliance/(h*h)), where C is the signed constraint error and w includes inverse-mass and rotational lever-arm terms. Increasing compliance reduces the correction applied for a given error. Decreasing h changes that denominator, which is why substeps and softness must be investigated together.

The lab uses an XPBD-style compliant position solver. The inspected joint routine does not retain the accumulated multiplier and its associated term from the full reference XPBD formulation. Do not claim that every behavior is invariant to iteration count or timestep merely because the method is called XPBD. The reference paper is useful background; the executable code and controls define this implementation's demonstrated behavior.

Substeps and iterations do different work.

More substeps repeat prediction and contact detection over smaller motion. More iterations revisit the coupled constraints within one substep. Their costs and effects are therefore different. Raising either number can improve an observed error while increasing CPU work; neither rescues arbitrary geometry, extreme speeds or an unsupported model. Use the phase timings and a chosen error measure to decide where to spend the budget.

The solver does not implement a general continuous collision detector. Its broad-phase expansion helps select potentially relevant pairs, but actual overlap contacts are still sampled. A fast, thin projectile can cross an obstacle between samples. Reduce speed or increase temporal resolution to investigate, and add a dedicated swept-contact instrument before making a tunneling guarantee.

Why face contacts and friction are separate.

A box resting on a face creates two corner contacts. The stock code solves an appropriate pair together using a small 2×2 system, reducing artificial torque from an arbitrary corner ordering. Friction is then applied after the normal pushes and, for a paired face, acts at the face midpoint with a Coulomb-style cap. This structure is an inspectable design choice, supported by the attached resting-stack controls; it is not a proof for every possible contact manifold.

Restitution combines the two bodies using their maximum coefficient plus the tuning offset. A ball with restitution zero touching a default ground with restitution 0.1 is therefore not a zero-restitution pair. The bounce threshold also ignores sufficiently slow closing contacts. Keep the pair's material rule and speed regime in a restitution claim.

Editable solver scope: editing JavaScript is executing trusted local code. The warm-up and fallback catch some exceptions and unstable outcomes; they do not form a general sandbox, prove numerical correctness or terminate an infinite loop. Keep the stock solver available and save experiments separately.

The ragdoll, jelly, streamers, buoyancy and water surface are educational approximations. Pills collide through a set of circles; the water is a one-dimensional height field with authored drag and buoyancy responses; the hero applies gameplay-specific forces. These choices keep the model understandable and playful. A different scientific task would need a different validation contract.

06 / The world is inspectable data

X3D makes the experiment
portable enough to question.

The level is represented as X3D 4.0 structure: a RigidBodyCollection, named bodies, collidable geometry, joints and ProximitySensor regions. The rendering bridge and the solver read a common plain-data model. This gives the world a useful textual boundary for inspection, editing, comparison and export.

LayerWhat it carriesWhat it does not establish
Standard X3D structureNamed nodes, body positions, geometry, body references, joint anchors and declared physics fields.That a different runtime executes this custom solver or reproduces its trajectory.
Flop metadataPrefab recipes, pill interpretation, collision groups, compliance, friction and other application-specific data.A new normative meaning for those fields in every X3D viewer.
2D simulationXY positions, in-plane angles, its own contact and joint rules.General 3D rigid-body behavior or a calibrated human model.
three.js realizationExtruded solids, camera, illumination, outlines, overlays and visual effects.That every visible decoration belongs to the physical collision model.

The source/export view describes the level specification and authored settings. It is not a serialization of the current complete game state. Bodies that have fallen in the running solver are not automatically new authored starting poses in the source model. Keep a level export, a visual review recording and a complete future simulation checkpoint as different artifacts.

A standard container can carry an explicit custom interpretation.

A Cylinder in the export can represent the visualization of a pill whose collisions are approximated by circles in the 2D engine. A ProximitySensor records a region that the game interprets as a star, checkpoint or fan. The standard structure remains useful, but a consumer needs the Flop metadata contract to recover the game's full behavior. A normal X3D viewer can inspect a starting arrangement without becoming Flop Lab.

This is an advantage when the loss is declared. Another tool can examine body IDs, dimensions, joints and regions; a review can point to a specific named object; a future adapter can map the supported subset deliberately. It becomes misleading only when “schema-valid” is presented as complete behavioral interoperability.

Import is a transaction boundary.

The current slice caps source text at 2 MiB, levels at 900 bodies, 2,400 joints, 512 regions and 250 prefab recipes, and coordinates at ±10,000 metres. A pill can expand to at most 128 circle samples; a water region can allocate at most 1,024 surface columns. These are explicit resource policies, not measured capacity or frame-rate promises. Inspector body-centre ranges must not be described as full shape or whole-motion bounds.

The upgraded importer validates the supported Flop slice before replacing the running world. Numeric fields need finite values and useful ranges; identifiers must remain unique; references must resolve; the accepted geometry and coordinate conventions must be explicit. Unsupported structures should produce an actionable refusal rather than a plausible partial scene.

Keep the original file when import fails. A repair that silently deletes an unknown body can preserve a green parser result while destroying the player's level. A robust authoring tool offers a scoped loss report or asks for an explicit transformation. The browser's Flop-slice checks and the separate pinned MCP schema/semantic checks answer different questions; neither should borrow the other's badge.

Prefab recipes and expanded bodies also need an ownership rule. A recipe describes how to rebuild; the expanded graph describes what is currently present. If a manual source edit changes only one, a subsequent recipe edit may overwrite that change. Inspect the source contract before mixing procedural and direct edits. The long-term editor should show generated regions and explicit overrides rather than hiding the relationship.

The useful abstraction is a boundary that preserves identity, meaning and loss.

What X3D adds to this particular game.

It provides a readable course artifact instead of making the world accessible only through renderer objects. It gives stable local names for inspection and diagnostics, typed structures that an independent validator can check, and a place to retain application metadata with the geometry. It also makes a second consumer possible: a layout viewer, a joint-graph explorer, a Blender import study, or a source-to-runtime selection inspector.

Those are opportunities, not completed bridges. Keep IDs, units, orientation conventions, geometry approximations and metadata versioning in each mapping. Rendering the same starting boxes is a narrower achievement than sharing a simulation. A future X_ITE review pane should explicitly show its supported view, remaining loss and source identity beside the running custom engine.

07 / Read the profiler with the right units

Measure the work.
Name the clock.

Simulation milliseconds measure CPU time for a fixed tick. They do not measure a display frame containing several catch-up ticks. Renderer milliseconds around the draw call measure CPU-side preparation and submission; GPU execution may complete later. FPS is an observed display cadence on the current environment, not a property of the X3D file.

Use four budgets: retained model data, active solver/event work, rendered GPU work, and human attention. A beautiful low-FPS overview and a fast unreadable scene fail in different ways. Quality controls change presentation cost; simulation settings change the experiment. Keep those categories visible when comparing runs.

A pose fingerprint is an early-warning instrument.

World.hash() applies 32-bit FNV-1a to every body's x, y and angle. It is a compact pose fingerprint. It does not cover velocities, controller state, joint history, water arrays, random-generator state or every gameplay flag, and it is not a cryptographic identity. Equal fingerprints are useful during a carefully controlled trace comparison; they do not independently prove equal models or equal complete states.

Compare model identity first, then the sequence of pose fingerprints. Retain engine source, parameters, initial conditions and the ordered inputs. The original attachment reports a Node/Chromium comparison on one machine after introducing portable trigonometric functions. That is a historical result with a named scope, not proof for all JavaScript engines, all arguments, future code or all hardware.

Review is not arbitrary-state resume.

The short history records body snapshots for visual inspection. A complete game checkpoint would also need controller timers, random state, current mode, hook/joint state, collected regions, mission state, cannon state, water state and simulation frame. The upgraded interface treats past scrubbing as review and returns to the retained live state rather than claiming that any past pose can be resumed as a deterministic game state.

The ghost is a separate presentation aid. It does not collide or drive the solver. Keeping review, ghost comparison and reproducible input replay as different features prevents an attractive timeline from acquiring unsupported authority.

08 / What was actually established

A useful result keeps
its boundary beside it.

The delivered archive's original checkpoint reports earlier Node/Chromium runs, math sweeps, schema findings and browser controls. Those records remain historical inputs. The current upgrade's fresh checks are recorded separately; read the final validation summary and machine/source identities delivered with the patch.

A fresh independent Node baseline reproduced 22 core assertions, 12 physical gates and six caught Node controls. The seventh original control belonged to a browser path and was not included in that Node rerun. A failed dependency, unavailable browser or crashed instrument cannot count as a successfully caught physical defect.

The upgrade also supplies tools/validate_levels.py, which runs the real pinned MCP validator on level/*.x3d with a bounded 2 MiB input allowance. The default 228,633-byte level passed XSD 4.0 and the implemented semantic checks using the upstream 4.1 UOM vocabulary, with 372 informational findings and no errors or warnings. Healthy positive input and deliberately malformed numeric/semantic twins distinguish a caught defect from an unavailable instrument. Read receipts/upgrade-x3d.json for exact file, source, schema and runtime identities; later level additions need their own fresh results.

The source-code reading and the teaching explanations in this guide are distinct from those measurements. The new mission thresholds are authored, and a proposed future adapter remains proposed. No paid model interaction, real-GPU performance, cross-engine coverage or formal Loom release acceptance is implied by a local play session.

For every promoted lesson: keep the symptom, exact specimen, expected diagnostic, repaired twin, instrument identity and known blind spots. A rule that distinguishes one bad contact case has earned that scope; it has not automatically earned every ragdoll, fluid or renderer.

A useful long-term Flop Lab is more than a pile of extra objects. It is a place where a player can ask what caused a stunt, change one mechanism, inspect the resulting graph, retain the experiment and turn a failure into a small reusable lesson. Fun supplies the motivation; explicit structure and evidence make the discovery travel.

10 / Follow the claim to its source

Read the code
that owns the behavior.

  • src/core.js: integration, contact/friction/restitution, joint corrections, fields, water, broad phase, pose fingerprint and snapshot scope.
  • src/level.js: prefab recipes, generated bodies/joints/regions and the authored seven-hall course.
  • src/game.js and src/expedition.js: hero control, ragdoll/stunts, missions, dash and visual ghost sampling.
  • src/x3dio.js: the application-specific X3D import/export contract.
  • test/ and the current validation summary: executed checks, negative controls, limits and scope.
  • Macklin, Müller and Chentanez: XPBD, 2016 — reference formulation; the lab's implementation must be compared with it deliberately.
  • X3D 4.0 RigidBodyPhysics component — standard node contracts; custom Flop metadata is a separate application interpretation.
