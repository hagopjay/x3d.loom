Footbridge Workbench

A 30 m steel truss footbridge, solved the way a bridge engineer would start: not "how strong is it under a full deck" but "where must the crowd stand to hurt each member most". The answer is a shape, one per member, called an influence line. Pick a member, see its shape, then walk a crowd across and watch eight members change from pull to push.

Stand one newton on the deck and note the force it puts in one member. Move it along and plot that force against where it stands. That plot is the member's influence line. A crowd is many newtons, so its effect is the area under the line where people stand.

What the mathematics says

8 of 37

Members that change sign

Under a full deck no member reverses. Under a crowd that stops part-way, 8 do: the four diagonals and four verticals nearest midspan. The midspan diagonal D5 sees 74.5 kN of pull and 37.0 kN of push, where a full-deck analysis shows only 31.9 kN of pull. That is 2.3 times too little, and no push at all.

line = shape

An influence line is a deflected shape

Lengthen one member by one unit, keep the rest rigid, and the deck moves into exactly that member's influence line. This is Müller-Breslau's principle. It was solved here from the transpose of the equilibrium matrix and agrees with the 37 lines found from unit loads to 8.9e-16. Statics and geometry are the same matrix read two ways.

0.996

The end post decides

Every newton on the deck leaves through the two sloping end posts. EP1 is the most used member at 0.996 of its buckling strength, so the bridge has 0.5% of spare crowd load before the first member is over. Chords are sized by the full deck, webs by the partial one.

span / 770

Strength governs, not stiffness

The full crowd sags the middle 39.0 mm against a limit of 83.3 mm. At a depth of one tenth of the span the truss is stiff with room to spare. Dead load alone sags it 17.9 mm, which a builder would take out with camber.

4.02 → 2.26 Hz

The crowd is heavier than the bridge

Empty, the first vertical frequency is 4.02 Hz and passes the 3 Hz line. The design crowd is 439 kg on every square metre. On the deck it weighs 19.8 t against 8.9 t of bridge, and the frequency falls to 2.26 Hz, inside walking pace. The rule checks the empty bridge. A packed crowd shuffles and does not march, which is the usual defence, and it is a judgment, not a calculation.

0 kN

The member that carries nothing

The centre vertical V5 takes no crowd load at all. Remove it and the top chord's free length doubles: its slenderness goes from 64 to 129 (limit 120) and its strength from 627 to 257 kN against a demand of 563 kN. It holds no load and the bridge fails without it.

3.18 to 4.51 Hz

Depth buys stiffness, not savings

From 2 m to 4 m deep the steel goes from 1,825 to 2,389 kg per truss, with no clear trend, because lighter chords are paid for with longer webs. Sag goes from 66 to 27 mm and frequency from 3.18 to 4.51 Hz. Choose depth for how the bridge feels.

1 wrong guess

What the study card got wrong

Before any run, the card predicted that 60 × 4 mm diagonals would fail on strength and on slenderness. They fail on slenderness only: the worst diagonal push is 37.0 kN against 40.7 kN. The prediction is recorded as wrong and the limit was not moved.

What this is not

Assumed, not designed

  • Plane model of one truss; the two trusses are assumed to share the deck load equally
  • Pinned joints, loads at panel points, stringers simply supported between floor beams
  • K = 1.0 with the member length as the buckling length: the top chord is assumed braced sideways at every panel point by overhead bracing that is not designed
  • No wind, no lateral frequency or lateral crowd lock-in, no fatigue, no temperature, no vehicle
  • No connection, bearing, deck, railing or foundation is designed
  • Sections have sharp corners; real HSS has less area
  • Eight of ten code lines are recalled or chosen, see codes
