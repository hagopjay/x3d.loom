# Why these ten: geospatial core and the last three volume styles (tracker batch 45)

Nodes: GeoOrigin, GeoCoordinate, GeoLocation, GeoTransform, GeoViewpoint, GeoElevationGrid, GeoPositionInterpolator, and the volume styles BlendedVolumeStyle, ComposedVolumeStyle, ProjectionVolumeStyle.
Rig for every number: Chromium 153.0.8010.12 on SwiftShader (no real GPU), X_ITE 16.6.0 (WebGL2), Babylon 9.32.0 (WebGPU). Pictures are 320 x 240.

## 1. Why these

Physics is paused by choice. The volume group had three nodes left and they share one renderer, so they went together. The geospatial group is the biggest remaining one and the rest of it (GeoLOD, the touch and proximity sensors, GeoMetadata) all depend on the seven here: a position in latitude, longitude and height has to become a point in the scene before anything else can use it.

## 2. Geospatial: what the nodes really do

**The earth is the scene.** A geographic position is converted to geocentric metres (the centre of the earth is the origin, x towards longitude 0 on the equator, z towards the north pole) with the WGS84 ellipsoid by default. Those numbers are about 6.4 million, and a 32 bit float has only about half a metre of resolution there. Everything the bridge computes is done in double precision and only the final small number goes to the graphics card.

**GeoOrigin is the trick that makes it usable.** With `rotateYUp` true the scene gets a local frame at the origin: +X east, +Y up (the ellipsoid normal, not the direction to the earth's centre, which differs by 0.19 degrees at latitude 45), -Z north. The coordinates become small metres around the origin, which a float can hold. Without `rotateYUp` the frame is the geocentric axes with the origin subtracted, which leaves the world lying on its side, and the numbers stay large. The pictures in the fixtures all use `rotateYUp`; the geocentric cases are checked as numbers (the placement matrix), because even with the origin subtracted a point 6.4 million metres from the centre is only good to half a metre after it goes through a 32 bit world matrix (logged gap).

**GeoLocation puts its children on the earth and turns them to stand up.** Its orientation is always the local up frame at `geoCoords` (east, up, south), whether or not the origin rotates. **GeoTransform is a conjugation, not a move to the centre:** the matrix is L^-1 M L (L the placement at `geoCenter`, M the node's own translation, rotation and scale), so with default fields it is the identity and the children stay where they were written. A reader of the specification who puts the children at `geoCenter` would be wrong for X_ITE 16.6.0, and the bridge follows X_ITE. A rotation of 90 degrees about the local up at a `geoCenter` 111 m east of the origin turns a box 10 m east of the centre into one 10 m north of it, which is what the fixture shows.

**GeoViewpoint** places the camera at the geographic position in the same east / up / south frame and applies `orientation` inside it, so the default camera looks north with up along the ellipsoid normal. X_ITE 16.6.0 follows the 4.0 schema: no `set_position`, `position_changed`, `geoCoord`, `elevationScale` or `navType`. A field of view outside 0 to pi quietly becomes pi/4. The navigation speed is (height above the ellipsoid, at least 0, plus 10) divided by 10 times `speedFactor`; the bridge computes it (checked as a number) but has no navigation mode that uses it.

**GeoElevationGrid** is a grid of heights in a geographic frame: `xSpacing` and `zSpacing` are in the units of the geoSystem (degrees for GD, so 0.001 is 111.32 m east and 110.57 m north), x runs east and z north, each cell is two triangles. X_ITE expands every vertex per triangle and averages normals without weighting, so the smooth shading of a ridge depends on how the cell was cut (the fixtures check the facets and the smoothed ridge, not the in-between). `yScale` does not scale the elevation of `geoGridOrigin`.

**GeoPositionInterpolator** does not go in a straight line in latitude, longitude and height. It takes a great circle about the earth's centre (about the GeoOrigin point when there is one) and changes the radius linearly. The middle of latitude 0 to latitude 60 is latitude 30.083, and 2.66 km **below** the ellipsoid, not (30, 0, 0). That is X_ITE's choice, not something the specification prescribes, and the bridge copies it because the aim here is the same numbers. Keys behave like any interpolator: a fraction on an inner key takes the later segment (so duplicate keys make a step), a short `keyValue` list is padded with its last value, and before any input both outputs hold the first key.

## 3. Volume: the last three styles

**ComposedVolumeStyle is a chain, not a sum.** Each child takes the colour the one before returned and replaces it. [red, blue] draws blue and [blue, red] draws red. The proof is a 2 x 1 table read after a style that returns grey 0.25: the lookup is at the incoming colour, so it picks the first texel after it and the second texel alone. Only composable styles are accepted, so a ProjectionVolumeStyle child is silently dropped.

**BlendedVolumeStyle** blends the colour in the chain with the second volume (read at the same normalised position, through the default ramp, then through the nested style): clamp(c x w1 + b x w2) on all four channels, alpha included. Because alpha is weighted too, the default weights of 0.5 and 0.5 halve a faint volume, and a volume under 1/255 alpha is dropped completely. **X_ITE cannot draw the schema weight names ALPHA1 and ONE_MINUS_ALPHA1**: the shader text has a stray space and does not compile, so the whole volume vanishes (the page also stopped answering for ten minutes when the rig held that case, so it is not in the fixture). ALPHA2 is silently CONSTANT there. The bridge reads the schema names (ALPHA1 = alpha of the first volume, ALPHA2 = alpha of the second) and keeps ALPHA0 as an alias; and draws any number of Blended nodes, where X_ITE draws none when two meet (a GLSL redefinition).

**ProjectionVolumeStyle is not a ray cast.** Every one of the 400 slice fragments of a pixel runs its own 16 step march along the viewing direction, starting 0.707 behind a point that is the slice position with its own offset *doubled* (a sign slip: a projection should subtract). The result is a grey with the alpha of that slice's own voxel, and the slices blend as usual. Consequences that look strange and are all measured: the front slice sees only 3 samples, so a uniform grey 204 volume under AVERAGE (always divided by 16) shows 38; MIN with nothing at or above the threshold stays 1.0 and draws white; MAX with a threshold stops one qualifying sample late. The bridge runs the same march per slice sample, so it reproduces all of it, and all 12 fixture volumes agree with X_ITE within 4 levels.

## 4. The smooth volume check (asked for by the showcase thread)

The cube fixtures of batch 43 could not show what a smooth volume does, so a 16 cubed Gaussian ball was drawn under Shaded, ToneMapped, Edge and Cartoon, with the headlight and with a directional light. **Unrotated, the two engines agree within about 8 levels on the lit styles** (Shaded 6/42/80/115/139/154/162 against 0/39/75/108/135/159/159 along a row; ToneMapped within 12). **When the Transform above the volume rotates it, X_ITE 16.6.0 lights it wrongly**: a ball looks the same in any orientation, the bridge's does, and X_ITE's changes with the rotation (rotation about Z by 0.9 rad moves the lit side; about Y it goes almost black; only a half turn is unchanged), which looks like the volume's rotation being applied the wrong way to the gradient (Suspected, cause not isolated). The bridge keeps the rotation independent picture (quality over the match) and the middle volume of `_volume-smooth-lit` is checked in the bridge only.

A second finding from the same test: **a smooth volume draws as a dim shell in both engines**. The alpha of a slice follows the voxel value and there is no correction for slice spacing, so the faint transition layer round a ball is already opaque before the bright core is reached, and the pixel shows the transition's dim colour: a ball whose core is 243 draws 18 grey, and a half-width block of 204 drawn from 16 voxels shows 70 (from 8 voxels, with nearest filtering, 204). Both engines do it by construction. A volume that is meant to look solid needs an opacity map that is steep at the bottom.

## 5. A test-rig slip worth remembering

Setting `GeoOrigin.geoCoords` from the test runner at first made the X_ITE picture vanish and then crashed the page. It was a harness slip: a plain list handed to a double field becomes NaN in X_ITE. The runner now builds an SFVec3d, and the test steps name the field type (the fourth element of a `set`). The list comparison also now treats a list of points and the same points flat as equal (the bridge keeps double lists flat).

## 6. Gaps (logged)

Geospatial: `WM` (Web Mercator) falls back to GD in X_ITE, and the bridge does too; the angle unit is not applied to geographic coordinates; the geocentric (no GeoOrigin) pictures are limited by the 32 bit world matrix; GeoViewpoint navigation (speed, `centerOfRotation` orbit) is not wired to a navigation mode; GeoElevationGrid normals follow X_ITE's unweighted average; a shear (non-uniform scale under a scaleOrientation) cannot be held by the node and is dropped. Volume: the three styles are tested with OpacityMapVolumeStyle as the nested style; the other nested styles use the second volume's gradient through the same function (tested by construction, not by picture); projection with unequal `dimensions` follows X_ITE's undivided direction; a changed texture below a Blended below a Composed rebuilds the shader (the touch walk is now recursive) but is not tested by picture.
